This study examines Salvatore Sciarrino's *Perduto in una foresta lontana* (1990)
from phenomenological, technical and political perspectives. Sciarrino's work marks
a radical departure from traditional composition by transforming silence into
musical material. Through techniques such as *sussurando* and *flautando* he creates
a phenomenology of breath that ties sound to the physiological limits of the body.

The work is also read against the political context of 1980s Italy, where radical
leftist voices were silenced: the *sussurando* technique stands for silenced voices,
and the final silence for irreversible loss. Combined with Merleau-Ponty's
phenomenology of the body schema and Heidegger's concept of anxiety, this produces a
musical language that turns the listener from passive observer into active
participant.

The title — *Lost in a Distant Forest* — is not merely metaphorical but a condition
placed at the centre of the listener's experience: an invitation to leave the
security of the concert hall and enter an unknown space.
