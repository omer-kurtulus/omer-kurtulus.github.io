Ömer Kurtuluş is a composer, music technologist and sound artist working across
instrumental, vocal, electroacoustic and multimedia contexts. His compositional
language is built on a single idea, developed in both his music and his writing:
**sonic archaeology**.

## Sonic archaeology

The contemporary composer works inside an over-saturated archive. What once
required years of travel and institutional access — historic recordings, early
studio works, spectral and microtonal repertoires, extended-technique practices,
mixed-media work — now circulates within the same searchable present. The
contemporary is not a thin slice of now but a thick, crowded time in which
historically distant practices are folded into one horizon.

Under that condition the compositional question changes. It is no longer *what can
I compose*, but *what does it mean to compose when so much has already been
composed, archived and made technically manipulable*. Sonic archaeology is his
answer: not archaeoacoustics, not the reconstruction of historic sites, and not a
nostalgic return to an imagined origin, but a stance that treats the inherited
archive of musical practice as a **stratified field to be excavated, re-situated and
re-authenticated**.

Two commitments follow. First, **re-authentication** replaces the vague appeal to
"respect for cultural origins": authenticity is not settled by origin alone but by
ongoing processes of re-placement and critical contextualisation — a shift from
culture-blocks to movements, relations and circuits. Second, the work proceeds by
**double excavation**: backwards, into the programs embedded in instruments,
techniques and institutions; forwards, into the future archives that current works
will themselves inhabit.

The composer, in this frame, is less a solitary originator of form than a **temporal
operator** — someone who navigates and reconfigures existing strata, gestures and
techniques under conditions of archival saturation and algorithmic mediation.

## In the music

This is a method, not a genre, and it is not tied to any single technology or to any
one piece. It runs through the notated concert music as much as the electronics:
through an instrument deconstructed to its physical anatomy until centuries of
tradition reduce to air forced through a pipe; through a traditional form excavated
for its rhythm and articulation rather than quoted; through ritual material
reassembled from civilisations that left only fragments; through a chamber work
built on a navigable corpus of the ensemble's own recorded playing; and through
multichannel work in which every layer sounds at once, separated only by height,
because nothing has to come first.

His engagement with AI belongs to the same argument rather than to a separate
technical interest. He distinguishes generative from assistive systems and works
with the latter as instruments of analysis and corpus-based listening — SoMax 2,
FluCoMa, RAVE, all inside Max/MSP — reframing the debate from within the ecosystem
of contemporary classical composition rather than from the vantage of mass
platforms.

## Biography

He was born in Istanbul in 1990 and studied at Mimar Sinan Fine Arts University
State Conservatory and Istanbul University State Conservatory, where he completed a
Bachelor's degree in Music Theory and Composition and a Master of Music in
Composition with Emel Çelebioğlu and Kemal Mete Sakpınar. In 2026 he completed a
second Master of Music in Composition at Bowling Green State University with
Christopher Dietz, Marilyn Shrude, Elainie Lillios and Piyawat Louilarpprasert,
where he was also a Graduate Teaching Assistant in the composition area. He now
works as a freelance composer and artist.

His music has been performed by the Toledo Symphony Orchestra, TAK Ensemble,
Ensemble Ipse, FLYDLPHN and Flam-a Duo, in venues including Kobacker Hall and Bryan
Hall at Bowling Green State University, the Great Lawn of Central Park in New York,
Süreyya Opera House in Istanbul, and festivals in Austria and Italy. He has been a
selected composer at the KEAR Electroacoustic Residency and the ISA Festival, and
holds prizes from the FIDELIO Piano Composition Competition, the International
Woodwind Festival and the Trieste Classica *Mediterraneo* album project.

Alongside composition he writes on timbre, phenomenology and the conditions of
contemporary practice — on Sciarrino's composition of silence, on Saariaho's timbral
form and auditory segmentation, and on timbre as a category defined largely by what
it is not.

From 2021 to 2023 he founded and directed ÖKMA, a composition-focused education
programme in Istanbul, in collaboration with Teknopol Istanbul and Istanbul Health
Sciences University — teaching composition to children from the age of five,
including students on the autism spectrum, as part of rehabilitation-oriented
projects, and founding the Spektrogram Vocal Ensemble.

## Areas of work

- Sonic archaeology — excavation, re-situation and re-authentication as compositional
  method, across acoustic, electroacoustic and machine-listening work
- Timbre studies, phenomenology of listening, and the cognition of timbral form
- AI-assisted composition and co-creative real-time systems — SoMax 2, FluCoMa, RAVE
- Corpus-based composition: corpus construction and live interaction with ensemble
  performance
- Rhizomatic, non-hierarchical musical structures — node-based, non-linear temporal
  organisation
- Ambisonic and multichannel spatial composition, up to 5th order
- Performer–system interaction and graphic-notation strategies
