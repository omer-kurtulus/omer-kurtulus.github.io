Ömer Kurtuluş is a composer working across neural audio synthesis,
electroacoustic composition and notated concert music. He grew up in Istanbul,
a city whose overlapping historical layers are the subject of *Strata*, an ongoing
album of works on the sound archaeologies of cities.

His recent practice centres on what happens when machine listening is placed
inside the compositional process rather than beside it. *STRATA II* runs three
RAVE models in continuous feedback over twelve loudspeakers, none of them trained
on the material they erode; *A Room For The Belated Sentences*, written for TAK
Ensemble, sets an ensemble against a navigable corpus of its own recorded playing.
Alongside this he writes acoustic music: *Animal* for orchestra, *Uzun Hava* for
solo tenor saxophone, *Focus* for the ensemble FLYDLPHN.

His music has been performed by the Toledo Symphony Orchestra, TAK Ensemble,
Ensemble Ipse and FLYDLPHN, in venues including Kobacker Hall and Bryan Hall at
Bowling Green State University and the Great Lawn of Central Park, New York.

## Areas of work

- Neural audio synthesis and corpus-based machine listening — RAVE, SoMax 2, FluCoMa
- Ambisonic and multichannel spatial composition, up to 5th order
- Acousmatic and fixed-media electroacoustic music
- Notated concert music for soloists, ensembles and orchestra
